Beneath the paint

When we acquired the portrait, the auction catalogue described it as a nineteenth-century work after Rembrandt. Cleaning brought an inscription to light. What can the paint itself tell us?

The portrait under examination. The X-ray on the examination screen.

From X-ray to paint sample

The X-ray shows where the painter built up the highlights with thicker, lead-based paint. Beneath the paint is the ground used to prepare the canvas. In a sample of this layer the laboratory found quartz and clay minerals, including kaolinite.

The expert’s assessment

In the preliminary report, the expert we consulted dates the portrait to the seventeenth century and places it in Rembrandt’s immediate circle. The ground beneath the paint offers a clue to the working methods of his workshop.

Rembrandt grew up by the Oude Rijn in Leiden. His father’s mill, De Rijn, stood opposite the family home and gave the family its name. Years later, Rembrandt painted The Night Watch on a ground of quartz and clay, probably river clay.
